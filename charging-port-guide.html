Why does my charging cable feel loose?

A loose connection can be frustrating. Before replacing a cable or assuming the phone’s port has failed, there are a few safe things to check.

General phone-care information · Reviewed 3 October 2026

IN THIS GUIDE What might cause it Safe checks to try What not to put in the port When to get help

If a Lightning or USB-C plug feels loose, does not sit properly, or charges only when held at an angle, debris is one possible cause. A worn or damaged cable, a damaged phone port, or another device fault can cause similar symptoms. A loose connection alone cannot tell you which one it is.

01

What might cause a loose connection?

Lint and dust can collect in a charging port and prevent the plug from seating fully. The cable connector may also be worn or damaged, or the phone’s port may need professional inspection. Repeatedly replacing a cable or port without identifying the cause may not solve the problem.

Start with the exact phone model and its maker’s instructions. Port design and approved cleaning guidance can differ between devices.

02

Safe checks to try first

  • Unplug the cable from the phone and charger. Turn the phone off before inspecting it.
  • Use a light to look at the port opening. Do not force the plug or push anything into the port.
  • Check the cable connector for visible damage, dirt, or bent parts. Follow the phone maker’s instructions for cleaning the cable connector, if needed.
  • If you see debris inside the phone port, cannot tell what is there, or it does not come away without inserting a tool, stop and ask a qualified technician to inspect it.
!

Do not scrape inside the port with a pin. A pin or other tool can damage the contacts or wedge debris further inside. Turning the phone off does not make inserting a pin safe. Do not add liquid or force the cable into place.

03

When to get help

Stop using the connection and get the device checked if the port or cable looks damaged, the connector will not fit normally, charging works only at an angle, the phone or connector becomes unusually hot, or the problem continues after checking the cable. If you suspect liquid in the port, do not connect a charger until you have followed the device maker’s liquid guidance.
